# Higher principal parts on a rectangle

## Result

For an axis-parallel rectangle whose four boundary lines avoid zero, this
slice proves that the closed boundary integrals of z^(-2) and z^(-3) vanish.
The same holds after multiplication by an arbitrary complex coefficient.

The proof is direct. On each horizontal and vertical edge it integrates the
explicit antiderivatives -z^(-1) and -(1/2)z^(-2), then shows that all endpoint
terms telescope around the rectangle.

## Why this matters

A third-order Perron kernel crossing s=0 has cubic and quadratic principal
parts in addition to its genuine simple-pole residue. Only the coefficient of
s^(-1) belongs in the residue sum. These theorems provide the exact formal
fact that the s^(-2) and s^(-3) terms contribute zero to the closed contour.

This module is generic complex analysis. It does not yet calculate the zeta
zero-pole correction or alter any explicit-formula theorem.
