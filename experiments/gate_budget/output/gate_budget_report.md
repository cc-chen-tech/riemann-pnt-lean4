### B2. Forced-successor exponent e_M and density conversion

The number of forced successors in a height window of size H = X^h
is at least X^(2 lam (beta-sigma) - gamma (q+theta)), i.e. exponent
e_M = 2 lam (beta-sigma)/gamma - q - theta relative to T = X^gamma.
The successor density in [T, T+H] is then T^(e_M - h).  With a
fixed separation delta, the per-node branching count is
q(T) ~ delta * density = T^(e_M - h), so the branch exponent is
k = e_M - h; hgap closes iff k > 0.

## C. Separated-capacity models and hgap closure

With window separation delta between successor zeros, cross terms
integrate to O(X^lambda/delta); the diagonal dominates only while
the successor count M satisfies M <= O(delta).

- C1 fixed delta: M = O(1), branching exponent kappa = 0, no growth
  (the fixed-gap regime, matching the known dead end).
- C2 dynamic delta(T) = T^kappa: M <= T^kappa, so the branching
  exponent e is capped by kappa.
- C3 (structural): branching count q(T) ~ local top-layer density
  times delta; density must grow super-logarithmically for kappa > 0,
  and the coherent energy mechanism supplies such density exactly
  when beta > 10/13 (e_M > 0).

hgap needs e * depth > q(sigma) with q(sigma) = 4 sigma (1-sigma),
so depth > q(sigma)/kappa.  Table for sigma = 2/3 + 0.005:

## Summary

1. All cubic design identities (A1-A6) verify numerically, including
   the shared-cap interval and the strict right/left margins.
2. Coherent capacity alone forces successor growth only for
   beta > 10/13 (both the e_coh and e_M formulas).
3. A fixed separation gap gives O(1) branching, consistent with the
   Lean no-go theorem for single-window certificates; growth needs
   super-logarithmic local top-layer density, which the coherent
   mechanism supplies exactly when beta > 10/13.
4. Decisive numeric questions for the gate: (i) cover beta in
   (2/3, 10/13] by a strictly better capacity model, and (ii) confirm
   the density-to-branching conversion in the toy iteration model.
